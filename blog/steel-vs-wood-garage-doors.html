Steel vs wood is the most common question I get when a San Diego homeowner is ready for a new garage door. Both materials look great and both last for decades when properly maintained, but they perform very differently in our local climate. Here is what you need to know before you choose.

San Diego presents a unique set of challenges for garage doors. Coastal neighborhoods like La Jolla, Ocean Beach, and Coronado deal with marine layer humidity and salt air that can corrode metal and swell wood. Inland areas like Santee, El Cajon, and Poway face intense UV exposure and temperature swings that fade paint and stress door panels. The material you choose shapes how much maintenance you sign up for and how your door looks ten years from now.

Steel Garage Doors in San Diego: Pros and Cons

Steel is by far the most popular material in San Diego County, and for good reason. A quality steel door with a factory primer and topcoat holds up well against salt air, UV, and everyday wear. Modern steel doors come in single-layer, double-layer, and triple-layer insulated configurations, so you can match both your budget and your climate needs.

Steel Advantages

Low maintenance. Resists dents better than aluminum. Paintable. Wide range of styles and insulation levels. Compatible with all major opener brands.

Steel Disadvantages

Can dent on hard impact. Bare steel rusts if the coating is scratched and left untreated. Plain flat-panel steel lacks the warmth of real wood grain.

For coastal homes, I recommend a galvanized or zinc-coated steel door rather than standard paint-over-steel, because the coating layer slows corrosion at the bottom panel edge where condensation tends to pool. Most major brands offer a galvanized option at modest additional cost.

Steel doors pair well with any garage door opener, and their consistent weight makes spring balancing straightforward. When a torsion spring eventually wears out, the replacement is simpler on a standard-weight steel door than on a heavy solid-wood door that puts more tension on the spring system from day one.

Wood Garage Doors in San Diego: Pros and Cons

Wood garage doors are the first choice for craftsman bungalows in North Park, Spanish-style homes in Mission Hills, and custom estates in Rancho Santa Fe. Nothing else matches the warmth and texture of real wood grain, and a well-maintained wood door adds genuine curb appeal to a high-end home.

Wood Advantages

Unmatched natural appearance. Custom styles, stains, and finishes available. Solid and heavy, which resists flex. Scratches and dents can be sanded and refinished.

Wood Disadvantages

Requires repainting or resealing every two to three years in San Diego. Susceptible to moisture and UV. Heavy weight increases wear on springs and hardware.

The key maintenance reality for San Diego is this: the combination of UV radiation and marine moisture cycles is hard on wood. Paint peels from the bottom panels first, where morning condensation sits longest. Once the sealer fails, wood begins to absorb moisture, which causes the door to warp slightly, which stresses the panel joints, which lets in more moisture. Staying ahead of that cycle means repainting on schedule, not when the damage is already visible.

A wood door is also significantly heavier than a comparable steel door, which puts more demand on your torsion spring system and opener motor. If you are switching from steel to wood, budget for a spring system check and adjustment to match the new door weight.

Which Material Is Right for Your Home?

Here is how I help San Diego homeowners make the decision:

  • Coastal neighborhoods (La Jolla, Ocean Beach, Coronado, Encinitas, Carlsbad): Steel, specifically galvanized or coated steel, is almost always the better long-term choice. The salt air maintenance burden on wood is real and ongoing.
  • Inland neighborhoods (Santee, El Cajon, Escondido, Poway, Lakeside): Either material works well. UV is the main concern inland, and both steel and wood hold up when painted with a UV-resistant coating. Wood can look spectacular on the right style of home.
  • Custom or historic homes (Mission Hills, North Park, Rancho Santa Fe): Wood is worth considering if the architecture calls for it. The maintenance commitment is real, but for the right property it adds genuine value and character that a steel door cannot fully replicate.
  • Rental properties or high-turnover homes: Steel. Lower maintenance cost over time and easier to keep looking sharp without specialized upkeep.

If you want the look of wood without the maintenance schedule, modern steel doors with embossed wood-grain patterns are a practical compromise. The texture reads convincingly from the street, and the care requirements look nothing like real wood.

Whatever material you choose, the hardware underneath matters just as much as the door itself. A quality opener, properly tensioned springs, and straight tracks determine how the door operates day to day. I include a full hardware check with every new door installation so you are not inheriting old problems on a new door.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is steel or wood better for a San Diego garage door?

Steel wins on durability and low maintenance for most San Diego homes, especially in coastal neighborhoods where salt air accelerates wood rot. Wood looks better on custom or craftsman-style homes but needs regular sealing and repainting. If you have a home near the coast in La Jolla or Point Loma, steel is almost always the smarter long-term choice.

How much does a new garage door cost in San Diego?

A new garage door installed in San Diego runs $1,200 to $2,800 for a single-car door and $1,800 to $4,500 for a double-car door. Wood doors typically fall toward the higher end of those ranges because of the material and finishing labor. Steel doors cover the full range depending on insulation level and style.

Does wood hold up well in San Diego's coastal climate?

Wood can hold up in San Diego but requires more maintenance than steel. The combination of UV exposure and marine moisture near the coast causes paint to peel and wood to expand and contract, stressing joints and hardware. A proper sealing and painting schedule every two to three years helps significantly. Inland neighborhoods like Santee or El Cajon are more forgiving for wood than coastal areas.

How long do steel and wood garage doors last?

A steel door in San Diego typically lasts 20 to 30 years with basic maintenance. A wood door can last just as long, but only with regular sealing, painting, and hardware checks. Neglected wood doors often show serious damage within 10 to 15 years in San Diego's climate. Steel holds its finish and structural integrity with far less upkeep.
