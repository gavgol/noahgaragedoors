What Is Included in the Installation Price

A complete new garage door installation covers more than just the door panels. Here is what every Noah Garage Doors installation includes:

  • Old door removal and disposal: The existing door, panels, tracks, and old hardware are taken down and hauled away. No pile of rusty parts left in your driveway or trash cans.
  • New torsion spring system: Every new door gets a properly sized and tensioned torsion spring matched to the door's specific weight and height. The spring system is one of the highest-stress components on any garage door and must be matched correctly to the new door to operate safely and last its full lifespan.
  • Tracks, rollers, and cables: All new hardware is installed on every new door job. Reusing old tracks on a new door is a shortcut that causes problems down the road, and it is not something we do.
  • Opener compatibility check and transfer or replacement: Your existing opener is tested with the new door. If the drive system is compatible and the motor is in good shape, we transfer it. If it is not compatible or is nearing the end of its useful life, we recommend a replacement and price it out before touching anything.
  • Full safety inspection and walk-through: Before we leave, the door is balanced, the sensors are aligned, and the auto-reverse safety feature is tested against both the force and photo-eye standards. We walk you through the operation so you know exactly how everything works.

What Pushes the Price Up or Down

The single biggest variable is door size. A standard single-car opening (typically 8 to 9 feet wide) uses significantly less material than a double-car opening (typically 16 feet wide), so the price gap between the two is consistent and real. Beyond size, here are the factors that move the number:

  • Insulation level: San Diego's climate is mild compared to most of the country, but an insulated door still reduces temperature swings in an attached garage. That matters most when the garage shares a wall with a bedroom, a home office, or a finished living space. Insulated steel doors cost more upfront but are the more comfortable long-term choice for attached garages throughout communities like Poway, Santee, and La Mesa.
  • Window cutouts: Windows add natural light and curb appeal but raise the door's price. Each row of windows adds labor and material cost. For coastal homes in La Jolla or Coronado, salt-resistant window seals and frames matter too, which affects material selection.
  • Specialty materials: Aluminum and full-view glass doors have become popular on contemporary homes in Carmel Valley, Del Mar, and coastal Encinitas. They are beautiful and low-maintenance but sit at the upper end of the price range because of the heavier framing and precision fit the design requires.
  • Header clearance and track configuration: Older garages in neighborhoods like North Park, Golden Hill, and parts of Hillcrest sometimes have non-standard headroom above the door opening. This may require a low-headroom track kit or a horizontal-only track configuration. It adds cost but is not unusual and is straightforward to handle with the right hardware.
  • Paint and finish upgrades: Factory-painted steel doors come in a standard palette. Custom colors or faux-wood finishes on composite panels cost more, though the price difference is often smaller than people expect. A faux-wood composite door can match the look of a cedar or redwood door while holding up far better to San Diego's UV exposure without regular repainting.

Choosing the Right Door Material for San Diego

San Diego's coastal climate brings strong UV exposure year-round and salt air for any home within a few miles of the ocean. That combination puts more stress on door materials than you would see in an inland or northern city. Here is how the most common options perform locally.

Steel (Most Popular)

The best balance of price, durability, and low maintenance for most San Diego homes. A quality steel door holds its finish well against UV, and modern galvanized and baked-on paint systems handle coastal humidity without constant upkeep. Insulated steel is our most common installation throughout the county.

Aluminum

Lighter than steel and naturally resistant to rust, making it a strong choice for homes within a few miles of the coast. Aluminum dents more easily than steel, so it suits lower-traffic garages better than homes where kids are playing near the door regularly. Popular in Ocean Beach, Pacific Beach, and Coronado.

Wood and Faux-Wood Composite

Solid wood offers the richest look but needs periodic sealing and repainting in San Diego's sun to prevent warping or fading. Faux-wood composite is a smarter choice for most homeowners: it looks nearly identical but handles UV and humidity better with far less maintenance over time.

Full-View Glass and Aluminum

Tempered glass panels in an aluminum frame look striking, let natural light into the garage, and require almost no maintenance. They sit at the top of the price range but hold up well in coastal conditions. Very popular on modern homes in Del Mar, La Jolla, and Carmel Valley.

If you are not sure which material makes sense for your home, I am happy to walk through the options in person. One look at your garage orientation, the sun exposure it gets, how close you are to the coast, and how you actually use the space tells me more than any chart can.
